How We Work

How We Handle Furnace Installation

Installation is where a furnace's lifespan is quietly decided. A condensing furnace produces acidic condensate that must be trapped, sloped, and drained correctly, and its PVC intake and exhaust must be sized, pitched, and terminated to the manufacturer's exact specifications; mistakes here show up as nuisance lockouts at best and flue gas problems at worst. Gas piping needs a sediment trap and a leak-tested connection. The return and supply must actually deliver the airflow the furnace expects, which our experienced technicians verify with static pressure readings rather than optimism, correcting duct restrictions while the system is open when the numbers demand it. Where a permit applies, we handle the process.

Commissioning is the step that separates an installation from an equipment delivery: we run combustion analysis to confirm the burners are tuned, set gas pressure to spec, measure temperature rise against the data plate and adjust the blower to match, verify the venting drafts, and run the furnace through full cycles while we watch. Then we walk you through the thermostat, the filter, and what the first season should look like. Done Right

Why Professional Furnace Installation Matters

What separates a good furnace installation from a bad one is mostly invisible once the job is done. The venting on a condensing unit has to match the manufacturer's tables for size, length, slope, and termination, or it will trip pressure faults and can put flue gases where they should never be. Gas lines need correct sizing, a sediment trap, and an instrument leak test. Condensate drains need real slope, because the acidic water keeps coming all winter and a flat line ends up as a midwinter lockout or a leak into the framing. Because none of this is visible in a finished photo, it is where shortcuts happen.

Equally important are sizing and commissioning. A furnace chosen by rule of thumb is usually too big, and an oversized furnace short-cycles its way through a shorter, harder life while keeping the house less comfortable. Whittier HVAC sizes from a real load calculation instead. After installation, our technicians commission the system with instruments: combustion analysis, gas pressure adjustment, temperature rise measured and held in the rated band by adjusting the blower, and a static pressure check to confirm the ducts and furnace are matched. That final hour of measurement that turns new equipment into two decades of quiet, dependable heat.

Prevention

Furnace Installation Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Use moderate thermostat setbacks; a correctly sized furnace prefers steady work to sprints
  • Change the filter on schedule from the first month; airflow is the new furnace's lifeline
  • Schedule annual fall service so combustion stays clean and settings stay in range
  • Save your installation records and model information so future repairs go faster
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on condensing models, especially before winter
  • Keep intake and exhaust terminations clear of snow, leaves, and nesting insects
  • Keep paints, solvents, and chlorine cleaners out of the furnace area
